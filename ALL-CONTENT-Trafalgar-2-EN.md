⚓ YOU DECIDE: genius or reckless? ⚓

October 21, 1805. Dawn off Cape Trafalgar.

You command 27 British ships. Ahead: 33 French & Spanish warships in one long battle line — including the Santísima Trinidad, 140 guns, the largest warship on Earth.

Your admirals expect the textbook move: form a parallel line, exchange broadsides, hope for the best.

Instead, your commander — Horatio Nelson — orders something insane: split the fleet into TWO columns and sail STRAIGHT AT the enemy's broadsides. For 40 minutes your lead ships can't fire back while 30+ enemy ships pound them. If the wind drops, you're dead. If the enemy van turns fast, you're surrounded.

It worked. 19 enemy ships taken. Zero British ships lost. Britain safe for a century.

But Nelson died on his own quarterdeck — and 450+ British sailors died with him.

**So judge him. Was the Trafalgar charge...**
