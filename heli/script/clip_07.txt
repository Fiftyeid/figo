Because a completely different idea beat them all. Instead of helping the rotor, why not turn it into a propeller? That's a tiltrotor. It takes off like a helicopter, then tilts its rotors forward and flies like an airplane, with wings carrying the weight. The US Marines have flown the V-22 Osprey this way for years. And in December 2017, Bell flew a new and much faster tiltrotor for the Army, the V-280 Valor. In testing, it reached 305 knots. That's about 565 kilometers per hour, or 350 miles per hour, faster than any helicopter has ever flown. The production version is designed to carry fourteen soldiers at more than 300 miles per hour. In December 2022, the Army chose the V-280 over Sikorsky and Boeing's Defiant, in what was described as its biggest helicopter decision in forty years. Then, in February 2024, it canceled its future scout helicopter program too, the one the Raider was built for, saying drones had changed aerial reconnaissance. The fastest compound helicopters ever built were left without a buyer. And in April 2026, the Army gave its new aircraft an official name: the MV-75 Cheyenne II.
