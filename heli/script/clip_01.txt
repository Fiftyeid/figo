Answer number one comes from Guinness World Records. According to Guinness, the fastest military helicopter on the planet is the Boeing CH-47F Chinook, with a top speed of 315 kilometers per hour, or 196 miles per hour. At first, that sounds like a joke. The Chinook is basically a flying school bus. It can carry around fifty-five troops, or sling an artillery piece underneath, and its basic design first flew back in 1961. More than twelve hundred have been built, and over twenty countries still fly them. So how does a flying bus keep up with attack helicopters? Look at the rotors. Most helicopters spend a chunk of their engine power on a small tail rotor, whose only job is to stop the body from spinning the opposite way. The Chinook doesn't need one. Its two big rotors turn in opposite directions and cancel each other out, so all of that power goes into lift and speed. But there's a catch. Not everyone measures top speed the same way. Some manufacturers quote true airspeed, some quote indicated airspeed, and some quote the never-exceed speed, a limit the pilot should never cross. On paper, a few helicopters beat the Chinook, depending on whose spreadsheet you read. Which brings us to answer number two.
