On the evening of August 11th, 1986, a British military helicopter tore across the flat fields of Somerset, in the south-west of England, at just over 400 kilometers per hour. About 250 miles per hour. That's not the strange part. The strange part is what happened next. Nothing. Forty years later, no conventional helicopter has officially flown faster. So what is the fastest military helicopter in the world? Ask the internet, and you'll get four different answers. A twin-rotor heavy lifter that first flew in 1961. A Soviet gunship nicknamed the flying tank. That British record-breaker. And a strange new machine the US Army just picked to replace the Black Hawk, which might not even count as a helicopter. They can't all be right. Or can they? To find out, we need to understand the invisible wall that every helicopter slams into, and the sixty-year race to break through it.
