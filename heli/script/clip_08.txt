So, what is the world's fastest military helicopter? It depends on what you call a helicopter. If you want the official title for a military helicopter in service, Guinness says it's the CH-47F Chinook, at 315 kilometers per hour. If you want the fastest helicopter flight ever certified, it's G-Lynx, at 400.87, a record that's now forty years old. If you count experimental compound helicopters, the X2 and Defiant went faster still, but never officially. And if you just want the fastest machine the Army will use to carry soldiers into battle, it's the MV-75 Cheyenne II, which isn't really a helicopter at all. Every one of them is fighting the same enemy: a spinning blade that can't go faster than sound on one side, and can't slow down without stalling on the other. As for the record holder, G-Lynx is still around. It sits in a helicopter museum in Weston-super-Mare, not far from the course where it made history, still waiting for a conventional helicopter fast enough to take its crown.
