5 lessons from Baghdad, 820 AD... for your business in 2026 📚🏴

Epic History's Abbasid documentary isn't just history. It's a 9th-century MBA:

**1. Build your own House of Wisdom.**
Al-Ma'mun didn't wait for geniuses — he imported and translated for them. You too: build your team's library, subscribe to 3 foreign sources, translate one article a week for colleagues. Imported knowledge = competitive edge.

**2. Pay the weight of books in gold — reward learning.**
The Abbasids made authorship worth more than gold. In your team: publicly reward anyone who learns a new skill. An employee who masters a new AI tool and teaches the team deserves more than a sales bonus.

**3. Choose your "Baghdad location."**
Baghdad was built where the Silk Road, India, and the Mediterranean met. Your project needs a crossroads too: where do audience + partners + funders meet? Don't build in the desert, then complain about no visitors.

**4. Paper before revolution.**
Science only exploded after paper got cheap. Today's translation: simplify documentation before demanding creativity. One organized Notion beats 10 scattered minds.

**5. Beware the curse of 861.**
Decline began when the court obsessed over guards and rivalries instead of science. Companies die the same way: more political meetings than product meetings. Every hour of internal war = a day behind competitors.

One-line summary, Epic History style: "Civilizations are built on knowledge — and fall to division."
