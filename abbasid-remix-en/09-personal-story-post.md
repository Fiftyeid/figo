**Ready-to-post text:**

Confession: I used to think the "Golden Age" was just a textbook cliché. 🏫

Then last night I watched Epic History's Abbasid documentary — all 47 minutes, no skipping.

At minute 12 I paused. Rewound. It was about Baghdad, 762:
A city built from nothing... a perfect circle. Palace and mosque at the center, markets around them, then houses, then walls. Its engineers calculated everything: winds, trade, defense.
I thought: they didn't build a capital. They designed an operating system for civilization.

At minute 25 came the second slap: the House of Wisdom.
Imagine a state spending a war budget... on translation. A Greek manuscript from Athens, a Persian one, an Indian one on mathematics — paying the translator its weight in gold.
Result? A man called Al-Khwarizmi writes a small book... that becomes the foundation of every phone in your pocket (Algorithm = Al-Khwarizmi).

I sat thinking about my small company, my kids' study schedule, this very account you're reading:
How much do we spend on "translation"? On importing knowledge? On books and courses? Or does our whole budget go to entertainment and appearances?

Then minute 40. The fall of Baghdad, 1258.
No 800-year-old fall ever hit me like last night's. Because the narrator said one quiet line:
"Power rotted from within... before the Mongols finished it from without."
It felt about us. About every team fighting over titles while forgetting the product. Every house consumed by quarrels while forgetting to raise minds.

I closed the laptop and decided 3 things:
1. One weekly "House of Wisdom" hour for my family: a book + discussion, no phones.
2. Translate one foreign article a month and publish it here, free.
3. Rewatch the series with my kids — history is the best leadership teacher.

If you're like me... thinking history is boring, try these 47 minutes. Link in the comments.
And tell me: which moment in history changed your view of life?
