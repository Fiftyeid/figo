> Platform: LinkedIn + Facebook | Length: long | Goal: saves & shares | Tone: Epic History documentary

---

In 747 AD, a black banner was raised in Khorasan... Just 3 years later, one of the largest empires in history collapsed.

This isn't fiction. It's the story of the Abbasids, as documented by Epic History TV in 47 minutes of maps, battles, and hard facts.

Here's the story in 3 acts:

**ACT I: THE TAKEOVER (747–750)**
The Umayyads ruled from Spain to the Indus — but a small Arab elite taxed non-Muslims heavily and treated new converts (the mawali) as second-class citizens.
The Abbasids — descendants of Abbas, uncle of the Prophet Muhammad — weaponized that resentment. Black banners against Umayyad white. Khorasan, Shia factions, and the discontented united. At the Great Zab in 750, it was over.

Lesson: Empires crack from within before they fall from without.

**ACT II: BUILDING THE WORLD (762–833)**
Al-Mansur didn't just build a capital — he built an idea: Baghdad, the Round City, "City of Peace" on the Tigris, where the Silk Road met India and the Mediterranean.
Then the masterstroke: the House of Wisdom. A state-funded translation project — Greek, Persian, Sanskrit into Arabic. Al-Ma'mun allegedly paid translators the weight of their books in gold.
Within a century:
- Al-Khwarizmi founds algebra (and gives his name to the "algorithm")
- Breakthroughs in medicine, optics, astronomy
- Paper replaces papyrus and parchment — an information revolution 600 years before Gutenberg

Lesson: Whoever funds translation and research owns the future.

**ACT III: DECAY, THEN THE STORM (861–1258)**
Central power dissolved: Turkish guards mutinied, governors broke away (Buyids, then Seljuks), the caliph became a religious figurehead.
In 1258, Hulagu's Mongols arrived. Baghdad fell after a short siege. The Tigris, it is said, ran black with the ink of thrown books.

Five centuries of rule — longer than most empires — ended when the state stopped doing what made it great: investing in minds and unity.

**3 takeaways for today:**
1. Well-funded knowledge becomes military and economic power within one generation.
2. Internal division is deadlier than any foreign enemy.
3. Cities aren't stone — they're networks. Baghdad was the ancient world's server.

Source: Epic History — The Abbasids: Islam's Golden Age (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O8hhwSn1iaU)

Decline began when the court obsessed over guards and rivalries instead of science. Companies die the same way: more political meetings than product meetings. Every hour of internal war = a day behind competitors.
