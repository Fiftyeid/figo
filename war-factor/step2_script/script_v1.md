# War Factor - "Which US Military Branch Has the Most Aircraft? (Air Force vs Navy vs Army vs Marines)"
Status: DRAFT v1 for approval (Step 2). Nothing is recorded or published.
Every spoken number is in ../step1_research/sources.csv. The ids are in brackets.

## Decision needed before approval: race basis
The main race uses manned aircraft, as agreed. No verified source gives a manned split for the Navy and Marine Corps, so they are NOT on the manned race bars. They appear as one combined card, labelled as combined, in the Navy section and in the closing answer.
- Option A (this draft): manned race = Air Force vs Army (both 2023). Navy + Marine Corps = one combined card (FY2022). Second view = total including drones: Air Force 5,286 vs Army about 4,600 (both 2023).
- Option B: total including drones as the headline, with a combined Navy + Marine Corps bar labelled "definition not stated in source". This breaks the manned-only rule for that bar, so I do not recommend it.
The verdict (Air Force first) holds under either option, because the combined Navy + Marine Corps figure (about 4,000) is below the Air Force manned count (4,959).

## [0:00-0:20] COLD OPEN
ON SCREEN: Title card. Subtitle: "Air Force vs Navy vs Army vs Marines". No insignia or logos.
VO: The US Air Force has the most aircraft of the four US military branches. In its 2023 count, it has 4,959 manned aircraft. The Army is the closest challenger, at about 3,900. The Navy and Marine Corps are reported together, and their combined figure is smaller than the Air Force's count. This video shows how each number was built, which source it comes from, and the year behind every one. It also shows what these numbers cannot tell you, because counting aircraft is harder than it looks.

## [0:20-1:00] THE RULES OF THE COUNT
ON SCREEN: "Manned aircraft, 2023" label. Rule card: "Numbers from sources.csv only. Year on every bar."
VO: Before the bars, the rules. The main race counts manned aircraft only, meaning fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters. Drones are left out of the race, because the Army's source reports drones separately from manned aircraft. Drones appear in a second view, with their own label. Every figure comes from a published source, and every source has a year. Where a number is rounded, I say so. Each bar shows its year, so you can check it against the source. Where a branch has no verified manned figure, it does not get a bar in the race. These rules come before any comparison, which is why some branches are missing from the main bars. Numbers that failed verification, including several third-party totals, are listed in a separate review file, and they stay out of the video.

## [1:00-2:00] THE COUNT
ON SCREEN: Air Force bar "4,959 | Air Force | manned aircraft, 2023 | Source: 2024 USAF & USSF Almanac (Air & Space Forces Magazine, USAF data)". Army bar "about 3,900 | Army | manned aircraft, 2023 | Source: CBO, September 2024".
VO: Air Force first. The 2024 USAF and USSF almanac, built on USAF data as of September 30, 2023, lists 5,286 aircraft in total. That total includes 327 unmanned aircraft and targets. Take those out, and the manned count is 4,959, including 25 gliders. Army second. The Congressional Budget Office reports about 3,900 manned Army aircraft as of 2023, most of them helicopters. CBO rounds its figures, so that number is approximate. Both figures are labelled 2023, which is why they can share a bar. The Air Force count follows the almanac's definition: aircraft assigned to operating forces for mission, training, test or maintenance, including primary, backup and attrition reserve aircraft. On the manned measure, the Air Force is more than a thousand aircraft ahead. The Navy and Marine Corps figure is from a different year, and I'll come back to it.
ON SCREEN CAVEAT (one line, also spoken in the closing section): "Air Force and Army: 2023. Navy and Marine Corps: FY2022."

## [2:00-3:45] AIR FORCE
ON SCREEN: Fighter/attack 2,093 (2023). F-16 family 862. F-35A 408. Bombers 141. C-17A 222. Helicopters 192. KC-135 family 376. T-38 family 495. T-6A 442. MQ-9 Reaper 244, marked as unmanned and outside the manned bar.
VO: The Air Force's five core functions are air superiority, global strike, rapid global mobility, intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, and command and control. Its biggest manned group is fighters and attack aircraft, at 2,093 in the 2023 count. The F-16 family is the largest single type, with 862 aircraft across the C and D models. The F-35A adds 408. The A-10C Thunderbolt II has 261, and the F-22A Raptor has 185. Long-range strike is a smaller group: 141 bombers, including 76 B-52H Stratofortresses, 45 B-1B Lancers and 20 B-2A Spirits. Mobility is a big block too. The C-17 Globemaster III has 222 aircraft, and the C-130J has 151. The helicopter fleet has 192, including 63 UH-1N Hueys. Tankers are the quiet part of the count. Fighters and bombers need fuel in the air, and the KC-135 family has 376 aircraft. Training is a big share too. The T-38 Talon family has 495 aircraft, and the T-6A Texan II has 442. Pilot-training aircraft count the same way as combat aircraft in this inventory, so they add to the total. The Air Force also flies unmanned aircraft, including 244 MQ-9 Reapers. Those are left out of the manned bar, and they are part of the 5,286 total.

## [3:45-5:15] ARMY
ON SCREEN: "Army manned, about 3,900 (2023, CBO)". Second-view card: "Including drones, 2023: about 4,600 (3,900 manned + 700 large UAS, derived)". Army roles: air assault; attack; reconnaissance; special operations; sustainment. Card: "about 80% of Army aircraft are manned rotorcraft (CRS, citing CBO)".
VO: The Army's aviation mission is to find, fix and destroy enemy forces through fire and maneuver, and to support combat with air assault, attack, reconnaissance, special operations and sustainment aircraft. CBO counts about 3,900 manned Army aircraft as of 2023, most of them helicopters. Its drone figure covers large unmanned systems only. About 80 percent of Army aircraft are manned rotorcraft, according to CRS's summary of CBO data. That is the figure used in the manned race. The Army also has about 700 large unmanned aerial systems, which CBO counts separately. CBO's report uses data from 2000 to 2023, and the 2023 figures are the ones used here. Since 2000, CBO reports, the number of manned Army aircraft has generally declined. Add the drones, and the total is about 4,600. That is the second view, and it is labelled including drones. The matching Air Force figure in that view is its total including unmanned aircraft: 5,286, also from 2023. The Army is still behind on both views, in the same year.

## [5:15-6:15] NAVY
ON SCREEN: Combined card: "Navy + Marine Corps (combined, FY2022): about 4,000. The source reports them together." No Navy-only bar. Role line: strike; maritime patrol; ISR; electronic attack; vertical lift; defense.
VO: Naval aviation works from aircraft carriers. Navy aircraft support carrier air wings with strike, maritime patrol, intelligence surveillance, electronic attack, vertical lift and defense missions. The Navy is the hardest branch to isolate for this video. I tried the Pentagon's original report, but it sits behind a subscriber login, so this figure is cited through a Congressional Research Service summary. Its aircraft are counted together with the Marine Corps, as one Department of the Navy total: about 4,000 aircraft for fiscal year 2022. The number comes from a Pentagon aviation plan that is not publicly released. Navy budget documents report a flight-operations subset, not the full fleet, so I did not use those either. I cannot split the combined total between the Navy and the Marines, so I won't estimate a Navy-only count. What I can say is that the combined figure is below the Air Force's manned count, so the Air Force stays first.

## [6:15-7:00] MARINE CORPS
ON SCREEN: No separate Marine bar. Note: "Marine Corps included in the combined Navy + Marine Corps figure."
VO: Marine aviation has six functions: air reconnaissance, anti-air warfare, electronic warfare, offensive air support, assault support, and control of aircraft and missiles. Its aviation provides fires, fire support and mobility to Marine forces. The Marine Corps does not get its own bar. Its published count is from 2022, a year earlier than the Air Force and Army figures, and showing it beside the combined Navy figure would count the same aircraft twice. The Marines are included in the combined figure, and that is where they stay in this video. Their fixed-wing and helicopter aircraft are reported in the same total as the Navy's.

## [7:00-8:00] CONTEXT
ON SCREEN: "FlightGlobal, 2026 World Air Forces Directory (public summary): just over 52,200 in-service aircraft, 161 nations." Definitions card: year | drones | trainers and gliders.
VO: How does this compare with the rest of the world? The best public figure I could verify is a summary line from FlightGlobal's 2026 World Air Forces directory: just over 52,200 in-service aircraft across 161 nations. The full directory, with country-by-country numbers, is behind a paywall, so this video does not rank other countries' air forces. Why do counts differ? Three choices change any aircraft number: the year, whether drones count, and whether trainers and gliders count. Sources also define an aircraft differently. FlightGlobal counts in-service assets, while the US figures here count total active inventory. A fair country ranking would need the same definition for every country, and no free source in this research gives that. The FlightGlobal total is a scale check, not a ranking. That is why every number in this video carries its year and its definition.

## [8:00-9:00] THE ANSWER
ON SCREEN: Final card, verified bars: Air Force 4,959 manned (2023) | Army about 3,900 manned (2023) | Navy + Marine Corps about 4,000 combined (FY2022). Second view: Air Force 5,286 | Army about 4,600 (2023, including drones).
VO: So, on the verified figures, the Air Force has the most aircraft of the four US military branches. In 2023 it has 4,959 manned aircraft, and 5,286 including unmanned ones. The Army has about 3,900 manned, and about 4,600 including drones, also in 2023. The Navy and Marine Corps together are about 4,000 in fiscal 2022. The year caveat, once more: the Air Force and Army figures are from 2023, and the Navy and Marine Corps figure is from 2022 and is a single combined number. What would change this? A same-year official count for the Navy and Marine Corps, if one is published. Until then, the Air Force stays first on every verified number in this video. In one sentence for the description: the Air Force has the most aircraft by verified count, and that lead holds in both views. If you count drones, the answer does not change, and that is the second view. Every source is listed in the description, with the figures I left out.
