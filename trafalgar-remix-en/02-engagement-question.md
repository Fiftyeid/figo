## MAIN VERSION (copy-paste) — "You are the admiral"

⚓ YOU DECIDE: genius or reckless? ⚓

October 21, 1805. Dawn off Cape Trafalgar.

You command 27 British ships. Ahead: 33 French & Spanish warships in one long battle line — including the Santísima Trinidad, 140 guns, the largest warship on Earth.

Your admirals expect the textbook move: form a parallel line, exchange broadsides, hope for the best.

Instead, your commander — Horatio Nelson — orders something insane: split the fleet into TWO columns and sail STRAIGHT AT the enemy's broadsides. For 40 minutes your lead ships can't fire back while 30+ enemy ships pound them. If the wind drops, you're dead. If the enemy van turns fast, you're surrounded.

It worked. 19 enemy ships taken. Zero British ships lost. Britain safe for a century.

But Nelson died on his own quarterdeck — and 450+ British sailors died with him.

**So judge him. Was the Trafalgar charge...**

1️⃣ 🧠 GENIUS — calculated risk, total surprise, decisive victory. Great commanders break rules.
2️⃣ 🎲 RECKLESS — it worked, but one lost gust of wind and Britain loses the war. Luck ≠ leadership.
3️⃣ 👑 NECESSARY — Britain was outnumbered; only a gamble could win. No risk, no Trafalgar.

Drop your number + ONE sentence of reasoning 👇
Best argument from EACH side gets pinned 📌 — and the winning side decides my next breakdown (the Nile vs Copenhagen).

## ENGAGEMENT PLAYBOOK
1. **First 2 hours = everything.** Reply to the first 20 comments with a follow-up question ("Interesting — what if the wind had dropped?"), not just "thanks". Replies double comment count.
2. **Pin your comment:** video link + "My take: genius in execution, reckless in risk — change my mind 👇" (a mild stance invites correction = more comments).
3. **24h later:** post results as a follow-up: "'Genius' won with 61%... but a naval officer in the comments destroyed us all 👀" + screenshot + link back. Turns one post into a 2-day arc.
4. **Cross-link:** "Loved this? My 8-slide Trafalgar breakdown is 2 posts back ⚓" — funnels debaters to the carousel (saves + follows).
