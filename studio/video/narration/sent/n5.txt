Men do this, on the last night of a world. They write down small things, because the large things are too large to write. The third tower has a lamp, and a jug of water, and three men who are not asleep. They are talking in low voices. Not about the army outside, but about a river. One of them is from a village on a river, and he is describing it: the way the water goes over the stones in spring, and the way you can catch a certain kind of small fish with your hands if you stand very still. The other two are listening. Nobody is saying: this is the last conversation we will have. Nobody is saying anything like that. They are just listening to a man describe a river, and in the listening, there is a kind of gentleness that is hard to put into words. This is what people do, when the end is near. They do not speak of the end. They speak of rivers.
