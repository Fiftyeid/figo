The first thing that reaches you is the smell. Woodsmoke, and horse, and wet canvas, and something cooking that you cannot name. The second thing is the sound. Not shouting. Not marching. The ordinary sound of a very large number of men doing very small things: talking, coughing, sharpening, laughing once, and then going quiet. The camp is laid out in lines. There are streets between the tents, and the streets are swept. There are latrines, dug in rows, away from the water. There is a place where the animals are kept, and a place where the food is cooked, and a place where a man can sit and have his hair cut, if he has something to trade for it. It is a city too. That is what nobody tells you about a siege. A siege is two cities, looking at each other.
