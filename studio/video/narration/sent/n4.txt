So they have developed a kind of rhythm. A watch, and a sleep, and a meal, and a watch again. They have learned where the wind comes through, and where a man can lie down without being seen by an officer, and which section of the parapet is warmest in the early morning. They have made a home of the wall. Let us walk along it for a while, from tower to tower, and listen. The first tower is quiet. Two men, both asleep. One of them is snoring, very softly, in that uneven way that is somehow more restful to hear than to do. The second tower is quiet too, but there is a lamp in it, and by the lamp, a young man is writing. He has a small piece of paper, and a stub of charcoal, and he is writing very slowly. If we look over his shoulder, and we will not, because it is not kind, we would see that it is not a letter. It is a list. A list of small things. The name of a street. The name of a bakery. The colour of a door.
