The defenders can stand on three levels. The attackers have to cross three thresholds. For a thousand years, this was enough. Tonight, the wall is quiet. The men on it are not standing in ranks. They are sitting. Some are leaning against the battlements with their helmets off, and their hair flat with sweat, and their eyes half closed. Some are mending. Some are eating bread and hard cheese, in the slow way of men who have learned that eating is a thing worth doing properly, even now. There are not many of them. This is the thing that no painting ever shows you. The wall is enormous. The garrison is tiny. Nine thousand people in a city built for half a million. They are spread so thinly along the line that in some places, a single man is responsible for a stretch of wall longer than a street. And they have been doing this for weeks.
