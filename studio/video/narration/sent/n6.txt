Now look out. Over the parapet, into the dark. You cannot see much. The moon is thin tonight, and the mist has come up from the water, and the ground in front of the wall has gone the colour of old iron. But if you are patient, if you stand there, and let your eyes get used to the dark, you will begin to see shapes. Tents. Hundreds of them. Then more, further off, and more beyond those, until the ground is no longer ground but a city of canvas, laid out in lines and rows the way a proper camp is laid out, because whoever commands it has done this before. And in the middle of that camp, there is a shape that does not look like a tent. It is longer than a house. It is darker than the dark. It sits on wheels as tall as a man. You will see it properly later. For now, let us simply notice it, and feel what the men on the wall feel when they look at it. They do not talk about it. They have stopped talking about it.
