The last tower on our walk has a lamp with a little door in it, and the lamp is turned low. There is a man sitting on the step, with his back against the stone, and his eyes closed. He is not asleep. You can tell by his hands. They are not loose. They are folded, and still, in the way of a man who is thinking. He is thinking about tomorrow. Not in the way we would think about it. Not with fear, or with plans. He is thinking about it the way you think about a long journey: the shape of it, the beginning, and the part in the middle, and the arriving. He has already packed. In his mind, he has already packed. And now there is nothing to do but wait. Let us leave him there. He has earned the quiet. Let us go back down into the city, and warm our hands, and take a cup of something hot, and let the wall be a wall for a little while. Because in the morning, the wall will be something else.
